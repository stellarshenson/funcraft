# Cult Mechanicus liturgical calendar - day plan

This plan gives the title and the technical fact of every day of the year, month by month. The writer turns each row into an entry as `calendar-brief.md` describes. The title is used exactly as written; the fact is kept true and is not extended. Days 1 to 5 of October carry the titles of `cult_mechanicus_calendar.pdf`; their rows give the theme of the PDF day and the fact joined to it.

## January

| Day | Title | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Feast of the Zeroth Epoch | The cogitators count time from the first second of 1 January 1970, and every training run counts from epoch zero. Begin the year as they do: a clean environment, a fresh seed, and the seed written down |
| 2 | Rite of the Three Partitions | Divide the data in three before any other rite: one part to train, one to tune, one sealed until the end. A split made after you have studied the data is a split already tainted |
| 3 | Vigil of the Idle Kernel | An idle kernel still holds every gigabyte it was given. Before you leave the forge tonight, shut down the kernels you no longer attend, so that others may wake their own machine spirits |
| 4 | Commemoration of Saint Holdout the Untouched | Saint Holdout kept the test set sealed for the whole project and opened it once, at the end. Follow the example: each extra look at the test data turns it into one more validation set |
| 5 | Observance of the Honest Baseline | Before the deep model is anointed, fit the humblest one: predict the mean, or the majority class. No score means anything until the logis know what the simplest method achieves |
| 6 | Feast of the Descending Gradient | Praise the Omnissiah for gradient descent, which finds its way downhill by small steps against the slope. Rejoice when the loss falls, yet remember that a low training loss was never the purpose of the pilgrimage |
| 7 | Rite of Device Selection | Name your GPU before you wake the framework: set CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES first, import second. A framework roused without instruction takes the first device it finds, whether or not another adept already toils there |
| 8 | Litany Against Leakage | Recite before every fit: no statistic from the test rows may touch the training rite. Scalers, imputers and encoders learn from the training partition alone and are then applied to the rest |
| 9 | Vigil of the Learning Rate | Too high, and the loss leaps about like a startled servitor; too low, and it crawls until the forge grows cold. Tonight, try three rates a factor of ten apart before you trust any one of them |
| 10 | Feast of the Dot Product | The dot product multiplies two vectors component by component and adds the results. It equals the product of their lengths and the cosine of the angle between them. Each output of a linear layer is one dot product of the input with a row of weights, plus a bias |
| 11 | Feast of the Blessed Checkpoint | A run that saves no checkpoint offers its hours to the void. Save the weights, the optimiser state and the step count together, for a model resumed without its optimiser state does not continue the same run |
| 12 | Commemoration of Saint Seedra the Repeatable | Saint Seedra fixed the seed of every generator - Python's, the array library's and the framework's - and her results returned unchanged each dawn. One generator left unseeded is enough to undo the others |
| 13 | Observance of the Steady and the Boastful | Some models state probabilities that can be trusted and some boast. Logistic regression is usually well calibrated, because it is trained on the log loss. Deep networks are often overconfident: they say 99 percent and are right far less often. Check the calibration before a probability drives a decision |
| 14 | Observance of the Added Variances | For independent quantities the variances add; the standard deviations do not. Two independent errors with standard deviations 3 and 4 combine to 5, not 7 |
| 15 | Feast of the Mean and the Median | Two servitors measure the centre of the same data and disagree. When a few great values drag the mean far from the median, report both and let the reader see the skew |
| 16 | Vigil of the Vanishing Gradient | In a deep network the gradient can shrink layer by layer until the earliest weights hear nothing at all. Keep watch with residual connections, careful initialisation and normalisation |
| 17 | Observance of the Squared Coefficient | A correlation of 0.5 sounds strong, but squared it is 0.25: in a linear fit one variable explains a quarter of the variance of the other. Square the coefficient before judging how much it explains |
| 18 | Saint Pinnia, Martyr of Unpinned Dependencies | She asked for the 'latest' version of everything, and one morning nothing ran. In her memory, pin every dependency to an exact version and commit the lock file beside the code |
| 19 | Observance of the Confidence Interval | A single number without its interval is a boast. A 95 percent interval comes from a procedure that captures the true value in about 95 of 100 repetitions; report one beside every estimate |
| 20 | Feast of the Sacred Embedding | Today we honour the embedding, which turns words into vectors so that nearness in space stands for nearness in meaning. Compare only vectors made by the same model; two models share no common space |
| 21 | Rite of the Morning Census | Begin the shift with nvidia-smi. It lists the three engines of BEHEMOTH, the memory each has committed and the processes holding it; know who labours where before you add your own load |
| 22 | Vigil of the Lone Outlier | One reading stands far from its kin. Do not delete it in the dark: first learn whether it is a broken sensor, a slip of the keys or the most important observation in the whole set |
| 23 | Rite of the Ordered Product | Matrix multiplication depends on the order: A times B is in general not B times A. The shapes must chain: a matrix of m by n times one of n by p gives m by p. Read the shapes before multiplying |
| 24 | Feast of the Attentive Head | Attention lets every token weigh every other token in the sequence, at a cost that grows with the square of its length. Give thanks for it, and be sparing with what you place in the context |
| 25 | Rite of the Channel Order | Image libraries disagree on layout. Most hold an image as height, width, channels; PyTorch models expect channels first. OpenCV loads colour as BGR, not RGB. Check the shape and the channel order before training; swapped colours raise no error |
| 26 | Commemoration of Saint Nulla the Sceptic | Saint Nulla taught that a p-value is the chance of data at least this extreme if the null hypothesis holds, and nothing more. It is not the probability that your hypothesis is true or false |
| 27 | Observance of the Scaled Feature | A column measured in millimetres will shout over a column measured in kilometres. Bring features to a common scale before any method that relies on distances or on gradient descent |
| 28 | Vigil of the Full Disk | Check the free space with df -h before any long job. A run that dies at hour nine because the disk is full is a preventable loss, and so is a disk filled with checkpoints nobody will ever load |
| 29 | Feast of the Batch | Larger batches give smoother gradient estimates and fill more memory; smaller ones are noisier and often generalise as well. Choose the size the card can hold, then tune the learning rate to match it |
| 30 | Rite of the Tokeniser | The model never reads your words, only the tokens the tokeniser cuts them into. Use the tokeniser the model was trained with; any other feeds it scrap-code it has never seen |
| 31 | Vigil of the Rare Fault | Anomalies are rare, and labelled anomalies are rarer. Anomaly detection therefore often learns what normal looks like from normal data alone, and flags what does not fit |

## February

| Day | Title | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Feast of the Regularised Weight | Weight decay asks every parameter to justify its size. Grant your model a little of it: the weights stay small, the function stays smooth and the validation loss stays honest |
| 2 | Rite of the Two-by-Two Table | On the second day of the second month we draw the two-by-two table: true positives, false positives, false negatives, true negatives. Accuracy, precision, recall and F1 are all counted from those four cells |
| 3 | Vigil of the Exhausted Memory | 'CUDA out of memory': the card has no more to give. Halve the batch, shorten the sequences or switch to mixed precision before you petition for a larger engine |
| 4 | Feast of the Scree Plot | A scree plot shows the variance explained by each principal component, largest first. Keep the components before the curve flattens (the elbow), or enough of them to reach a stated share of the variance, such as 95 percent |
| 5 | Commemoration of Saint Strata the Even-Handed | Saint Strata divided the data so that every fold held the rare class in the same proportion as the whole. Stratify your splits, or one fold may contain no positive examples at all |
| 6 | Feast of the Warm-Up | A cold engine is not thrown at once into full burn. Raise the learning rate gently over the first steps, while the optimiser's running statistics are still unreliable, and only then let it run at full strength |
| 7 | Rite of the Natural Base | Second month, seventh day: 2.7, the opening digits of e. It is usually the base of the logarithm in the cross-entropy loss, and the base of the exponential in every softmax |
| 8 | Observance of the Sampling Bias | The data you could collect is not the data that exists. Ask today who or what never made it into the table, for a model inherits every blind spot of its sample |
| 9 | Feast of the Surrogate Model | Bayesian optimisation of hyperparameters fits a cheap model, the surrogate, to the scores of the trials run so far. The surrogate predicts the score of untried settings and how uncertain that prediction is, and the next trial is chosen from it. It pays off when each trial is expensive |
| 10 | Feast of the Falling Loss | Plot the training and validation loss on the same axes and read them as a logis reads an augury. Falling together is health; training falling while validation climbs is the first sign of overfitting |
| 11 | Rite of the Many Hypotheses | Test twenty true null hypotheses at the 5 percent level and expect one false discovery. Count how many comparisons you ran today, and correct the threshold before you announce a miracle |
| 12 | Rite of the Ranked Pair | Rank correlation (Spearman's coefficient) is the ordinary correlation computed on the ranks of the values. It detects any relation that only rises or only falls, straight or curved, and one extreme value moves it little |
| 13 | Commemoration of Saint Calibra the Well-Tempered | Of all the times Saint Calibra's model said 70 percent, it was right seven times in ten. Check your own with a reliability plot, for accuracy and calibration are separate virtues |
| 14 | Feast of the Sharpened Prompt | Tell the model who the reader is, what the output is for and what a good answer looks like. A prompt written as a clear brief to a capable colleague outperforms any incantation of magic words |
| 15 | Vigil of the Shared Vault | What is placed in @volumes/shared is seen by every adept of the forge. Put a dataset there once instead of copying it ten times, and never leave a secret where all may read it |
| 16 | Observance of the Expected Calibration Error | To measure calibration, sort the predictions into bins by confidence. In each bin compare the average confidence with the share of correct answers. The expected calibration error is the average gap, weighted by bin size. The value depends on the number of bins, so state it |
| 17 | Observance of the Unseen Class | When one case in a hundred is positive, a model that always answers 'no' scores 99 percent accuracy and finds nothing. On imbalanced data, honour precision, recall and the precision-recall curve instead |
| 18 | Feast of the Half-Precision Float | Mixed precision runs most operations in 16-bit floats and keeps the sensitive ones in 32-bit. Memory falls and speed rises; leave the gradient scaler on, lest small gradients underflow to zero |
| 19 | Commemoration of Saint Epsilona the Noise-Bearer | The reparametrisation trick. A gradient cannot pass through a random draw. Epsilona wrote the sample as the mean plus the standard deviation times a noise value drawn from a standard normal. The randomness sits in the noise alone, so the gradient reaches the mean and the standard deviation |
| 20 | Vigil of the Early Stop | Keep watch over the validation loss. When it has not improved for a set number of epochs, halt the rite and restore the best checkpoint; further training would only teach the noise |
| 21 | Rite of the Prior and the Posterior | State what you believed before the data, then let the evidence update it. With little data the prior rules the posterior; with much data the evidence overrules it. Know which regime you are in |
| 22 | Observance of the Removed Part | An ablation removes one component at a time and measures again. If the score does not fall, the part was ornament, and the table tells the Magos which of your additions earn their place |
| 23 | Feast of the Unturned Vector | An eigenvector of a matrix is a vector that the matrix only stretches and does not turn; the eigenvalue is the stretch factor. The eigenvectors of a covariance matrix are the principal components of the data |
| 24 | Feast of the Overlapping Boxes | Intersection over union compares a predicted box with the true box: the area they share divided by the area they cover together. 1 is a perfect match. A detection is commonly counted as correct at 0.5 or more |
| 25 | Vigil of the Context Window | The model attends only to what fits inside its context window; whatever falls outside does not exist for it. Tonight, measure your prompt in tokens, not in characters or in hope |
| 26 | Observance of the Curse of Dimensions | As dimensions multiply, the data grows sparse and every point drifts far from every other. A method that leans on nearest neighbours in three dimensions may find no near neighbours at all in three thousand |
| 27 | Commemoration of Saint Tempera the Cool-Headed | Temperature scaling. Tempera divided the logits of an overconfident network by one number, the temperature, which she fitted on held-out data. A temperature above 1 softens the probabilities. The predicted class does not change, so the accuracy stays the same |
| 28 | Feast of the Isolated Point | An isolation forest cuts the data with random splits. An anomaly sits apart, so few splits isolate it; a normal point needs many. A short path to isolation means a likely anomaly |
| 29 | Observance of the Intercalary Day | This day exists only in leap years, and code that assumes 365 days or a 28-day February fails upon it. Never do calendar arithmetic by hand: use a date library, and test it on 29 February |

## March

| Day | Title | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rite of the First Look | Before any model, look at the data itself: twenty raw rows, the range of each column, the count of nulls. Many of a project's errors are visible on the first page, if anyone reads it |
| 2 | Rite of the Expected Improvement | In Bayesian optimisation an acquisition function chooses the next trial. It weighs settings where the surrogate predicts a good score against settings where the surrogate is uncertain. Expected improvement is a common choice: how much better than the best score so far a setting is expected to be |
| 3 | Observance of the Sliding Kernel | One small set of weights, slid across the whole image: that is a convolution. A pattern learned in one corner is recognised in every other, and the sharing keeps the network small. Honour this thrift |
| 4 | Vigil of the Exploding Gradient | When the loss turns to NaN in a single step, a gradient has grown beyond all measure. Clip the gradient norm, lower the learning rate and inspect the batch that set it off |
| 5 | Commemoration of Saint Laplacia the Connected | The graph Laplacian. Laplacia subtracted the adjacency matrix of a graph from the diagonal matrix of node degrees. The smallest eigenvalue of this matrix is always zero, and the number of zero eigenvalues equals the number of separate connected parts of the graph |
| 6 | Rite of the Undivided Group | When one patient, one customer or one machine supplies many rows, all of those rows belong on the same side of the split. Split by group, or the model will be examined on subjects it has already met |
| 7 | Rite of the Centred Cloud | PCA starts by subtracting the mean of each feature. Without centring, the first component points from the origin toward the mean of the data and says nothing about its spread. A PCA class centres the data for you; a plain SVD does not |
| 8 | Feast of the Holy Residual Connection | One of the holy truths of the cult. A residual connection adds the output of a block to its input. In a transformer the chain of these connections forms the residual stream: every block reads from it and adds its result back to it. It is the working memory of the model, the one channel through which all layers pass information, and the clear path by which the gradient reaches the first layers |
| 9 | Vigil of the Train-Serve Skew | A feature computed by one piece of code in training and by another in production differs in small ways, and the model degrades without an error. Use the same code for both, and compare the feature values of the two on the same records |
| 10 | Observance of the Rank | The rank of a matrix is the number of independent directions in it. A matrix of 1,000 by 1,000 with rank 8 can be written as the product of two thin matrices, 1,000 by 8 and 8 by 1,000: 16,000 numbers in place of a million |
| 11 | Observance of the Survivors | The table lists only the customers who stayed, the machines that still run, the studies that were published. Ask what became of the absent ones before you conclude anything from those that remain |
| 12 | Commemoration of Saint Evalia the Mode-Switcher | Saint Evalia never forgot to put the model in evaluation mode before measuring it. Dropout left active and batch statistics left updating will quietly distort every validation score |
| 13 | Rite of the Adaptive Moment | The Adam optimiser keeps a running mean of each gradient and of its square, and scales every step by them. It forgives a poorly chosen learning rate more readily than plain descent, though never entirely |
| 14 | Feast of the Unending Ratio | Third month, fourteenth day: 3.14, the first digits of pi. Scatter random points in a square, count the share that fall inside the inscribed circle, multiply by four: a Monte Carlo rite that converges on it, slowly |
| 15 | Observance of the Reversed Aggregate | Simpson's paradox: a trend that holds in every subgroup can reverse when the groups are pooled. Before you report the total, look at the strata, and ask which grouping the question is really about |
| 16 | Feast of the Multilayer Perceptron | A multilayer perceptron is a stack of layers; each computes weighted sums of its inputs and passes them through a non-linear function. Without the non-linear function the whole stack collapses into a single linear map, however many layers it has |
| 17 | Vigil of the Hallucination | Plausible is not the same as true, and a language model is trained to produce the plausible. Tonight, check every citation, number and name it gave you against a source you can open |
| 18 | Feast of the Central Limit | Average enough independent readings of finite variance and their mean settles toward a bell curve, whatever shape the readings had. On this one theorem rest most of the error bars you have ever drawn |
| 19 | Rite of the Borrowed Mean | A pretrained vision model expects its input normalised with the mean and standard deviation of the data it was trained on, usually those of ImageNet. With other values it still runs, raises no error and is less accurate |
| 20 | Commemoration of Saint Imputa the Gap-Filler | Saint Imputa filled each gap with the median and always added a column recording that she had. Impute if you must, but keep the flag: that a value was missing is often a signal in itself |
| 21 | Rite of the Balanced Trade | A rigid model errs the same way every time: bias. A flexible one errs differently on every sample: variance. Total error is lowest somewhere between, so find out today which side of that point you stand on |
| 22 | Feast of Random Silence | Dropout silences a random share of activations at each training step, so that no single unit becomes indispensable. At inference every unit speaks; the scaling applied in training keeps the expected totals equal |
| 23 | Rite of the Split Before Augmentation | Split the data first, then augment or oversample the training part only. If augmented copies of one image, or synthetic neighbours of one row, land on both sides of the split, the validation score is inflated |
| 24 | Feast of the Small Sample | The mean of a small sample with unknown variance follows Student's t distribution, which has heavier tails than the normal distribution. Intervals from few observations are therefore wider. Above about 30 observations the two are almost the same |
| 25 | Rite of the Few Examples | Show the model three worked examples of the output you want and it will follow their form more faithfully than any description. Vary the examples, or it will imitate the one you gave too closely |
| 26 | Feast of the Standard Error | The standard deviation describes the spread of the data; the standard error describes the uncertainty of the mean, and shrinks with the square root of the sample size. Say which one your error bars show |
| 27 | Commemoration of Saint Robusta the Median-Minded | A z-score uses the mean and the standard deviation, and the outliers you look for inflate both. Robusta used the median and the median absolute deviation instead, which a few extreme values barely move |
| 28 | Observance of the Duplicated Row | The same record on both sides of the split inflates every score. Remove duplicates before you divide the data: exact copies first, then the near-copies that differ by a space or a capital letter |
| 29 | Vigil of the Shifting Distribution | Compare this month's inputs with the data the model was trained on. The world that produced the training set does not stand still, and a model degrades in silence when the two drift apart |
| 30 | Rite of the Logit | Logits are raw scores; probabilities come only after the softmax. Give each loss function the one it expects; the framework usually raises no error when handed the other, and the model simply trains badly |
| 31 | Feast of the Dense Region | DBSCAN groups the points that lie in dense regions and marks points in sparse regions as noise. It needs no number of clusters in advance and finds clusters of any shape. It needs a radius and a minimum number of neighbours |

## April

| Day | Title | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Observance of the Too-Good Score | A validation accuracy of 99.9 percent on a hard problem is no triumph; it is a symptom. Suspect leakage first, a duplicated row second, a fault in the metric third, and your own brilliance last |
| 2 | Rite of the Cosine Descent | The lexmechanics chart the learning rate as a slow descent, by steps or along a cosine curve. Held constant, it ends the run still rattling around the minimum; decayed, it settles |
| 3 | Feast of the Good and Bad Trials | The tree-structured Parzen estimator, the default sampler of Optuna, splits the finished trials into a good group and a bad group and models the hyperparameters of each. It proposes values that are likely in the good group and unlikely in the bad one. It handles categorical and conditional hyperparameters well |
| 4 | Rite of All but the Top | Word embeddings share a common mean vector and a few dominant directions that mostly encode word frequency, not meaning. The all-but-the-top method subtracts the mean and removes the top few principal components. Similarity between words then becomes more meaningful |
| 5 | Commemoration of Saint Solva the Uninverting | To solve the system A x = b, Solva never computed the inverse of A. She called a solver, which is faster and loses less precision. In NumPy that is solve(A, b), not inv(A) times b |
| 6 | Observance of the Sufficient Sample | The logis will not bless an experiment too small to detect the effect it seeks; it wastes even the samples it does use. Estimate the sample size before you begin, from the smallest effect that would matter |
| 7 | Rite of the Zeroed Gradient | In PyTorch, gradients accumulate across backward passes until they are cleared. Call zero_grad in every training step, before the backward pass, or the model is steered by the sum of every batch it has seen |
| 8 | Feast of the Retrieved Passage | Retrieval lets a model answer from documents it was never trained on, by placing the relevant passages in its context. The answer can be no better than what was retrieved, so evaluate the search before the prose |
| 9 | Rite of the Transformer Block | A transformer is a stack of identical blocks. Each block holds a self-attention layer and a small feed-forward network, and each of the two is wrapped in a residual connection with a layer normalisation. Attention moves information between tokens; the feed-forward network works on each token alone |
| 10 | Observance of the Returning Mean | Last month's worst performers will tend to improve and its best to decline, with no intervention at all. Before you credit your fix, ask whether extreme values were merely drifting back toward the average |
| 11 | Rite of the Frozen Layers | When data is scarce, freeze the pretrained body and train only the new head. Thaw the deeper layers later and with a smaller learning rate, lest you overwrite what was learned at great expense |
| 12 | Commemoration of Saint Lettera the Unsquashed | Resizing an image to a square without keeping its aspect ratio squashes every shape. Lettera kept the ratio and padded the rest (letterboxing), and she resized in the same way in training and in inference |
| 13 | Feast of the Random Forest | Many trees, each grown on a different bootstrap sample and a random subset of features, vote together. Their errors are partly independent, so the forest is steadier than any single tree within it |
| 14 | Observance of the Peeked Result | Checking a running experiment daily and stopping the moment it looks significant pushes the false-positive rate far above 5 percent. Fix the sample size in advance, or use a test designed for repeated looks |
| 15 | Vigil of the Silent NaN | One NaN poisons every sum and mean it touches, and it is not even equal to itself. Tonight, assert that your features and your loss are finite, before the corruption spreads through the whole data-shrine |
| 16 | Rite of the Suppressed Twin | An object detector proposes many overlapping boxes for one object. Non-maximum suppression keeps the box with the highest score and removes the others that overlap it more than a threshold |
| 17 | Feast of the Bootstrap | Resample your data with replacement a thousand times, recompute the statistic each time, and the spread of the results estimates its uncertainty. No formula is required, only patience and a willing cogitator |
| 18 | Observance of the Encoded Category | A category is not a number: code red, green and blue as 1, 2 and 3 and a linear model concludes that blue is three times red. Where no order exists, use one-hot columns or a learned embedding |
| 19 | Commemoration of Saint Annota the Consistent | Saint Annota wrote the labelling guideline before the first label and measured how often two annotators agreed. Where they disagreed she amended the guideline, never the annotators |
| 20 | Rite of the Accumulated Step | When the batch you want does not fit in memory, run several small batches and step the optimiser only after their gradients have accumulated. The update approximates the large batch at the memory cost of the small |
| 21 | Vigil of the Small-Batch Norm | Batch normalisation estimates the mean and the variance from the batch. With very small batches those estimates are noisy and training suffers. Use group normalisation or layer normalisation there; they do not depend on the batch size |
| 22 | Feast of the Principal Component | Principal components are the directions of greatest variance, each orthogonal to the last. Standardise the features first, or the leading component will merely point at whichever column has the largest units |
| 23 | Observance of the Weighted Average | The mean of group means is not the overall mean unless the groups are the same size. Weight each group by its count, or a group of three will speak as loudly as a group of three thousand |
| 24 | Rite of the Structured Reply | When a language model must return JSON, state the schema, show an example and validate the reply in code before anything consumes it. Trust the parser, never the appearance of the text |
| 25 | Vigil of the Arrow of Time | For data ordered in time, train on the past and validate on the future, never the reverse. A random shuffle lets the model glimpse tomorrow, and its forecast scores become fiction |
| 26 | Feast of the Variational Autoencoder | A variational autoencoder encodes each input as a mean and a variance, not as one point, and decodes a sample drawn from them. Its loss adds the reconstruction error and a term that keeps the codes close to a standard normal. New data is then generated by decoding random normal vectors |
| 27 | Commemoration of Saint Loada the Interpreter | Each principal component is a weighted mix of the original features; the weights are the loadings. Loada read them to learn which features drive a component. The sign of a component is arbitrary: the whole component may be flipped |
| 28 | Observance of the Effect Size | With enough data, a difference too small to matter becomes statistically significant, and the logis are not impressed. Report how large the effect is, with its interval, and whether it is worth acting upon |
| 29 | Rite of the Padding Mask | Sequences in a batch are padded to equal length, and the padding means nothing. Pass the attention mask along with them, or the model will attend to emptiness and blend it into the answer |
| 30 | Vigil of the Personal Reliquary | Your own work belongs in @volumes/personal, under your own name and in your own order. Tonight, gather the stray files from temporary corners and carry them to their proper reliquary |

## May

| Day | Title | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Feast of the Chain Rule | Backpropagation is the chain rule applied from the loss back to the first layer, reusing each intermediate result on the way. Every network you have trained by gradient descent rests on this one piece of calculus |
| 2 | Rite of the Shuffled Deck | Shuffle the training data anew each epoch. Batches served in a fixed order, or sorted by label, feed the optimiser a biased sequence of gradients, and the descent goes astray |
| 3 | Observance of the Base Rate | A test that is 99 percent accurate, applied to a condition found in one case per ten thousand, raises mostly false alarms. Always ask how common the thing was before the evidence arrived |
| 4 | Feast of the Brier Score | The Brier score is the mean squared difference between the predicted probability and the outcome, 0 or 1. Lower is better. A forecast of 0.5 for everything scores 0.25. It rewards probabilities that are both calibrated and decisive |
| 5 | Feast of the Five Folds | Fifth day, fifth month: five folds. Train five times, each time holding out a different fifth, and report the mean and the spread of the five scores, not merely the best of them |
| 6 | Commemoration of Saint Isotropa the Even-Spread | Isotropa noticed that the embeddings of a language model often fill only a narrow cone of the space, so any two of them have a high cosine similarity. She compared similarities with each other, or centred the embeddings first, and never read a raw cosine of 0.8 as 'very similar' |
| 7 | Rite of the Lowered Temperature | Temperature divides the logits before sampling: below one, the model keeps to its likeliest tokens; above one, it wanders. Keep it low for extraction and code, and let it rise only for invention |
| 8 | Observance of the Confounder | Ice-cream sales and sunburn rise together, yet neither causes the other: the summer sun drives both. Before you claim that X moves Y, search for the third variable that moves them both |
| 9 | Feast of the Decision Tree | A tree asks one question at a time and can be read aloud to a sceptical Magos. Left unpruned, it grows a leaf for every training row; limit its depth, or bind it into an ensemble |
| 10 | Rite of the Pruned Trial | Most hyperparameter settings show early that they are poor. Successive halving gives many settings a small budget, keeps the best fraction and gives those more, again and again. Far more settings are tried for the same total compute |
| 11 | Observance of the Three Norms | The L2 norm of a vector is its straight-line length. The L1 norm is the sum of the absolute values of its components. The maximum norm is its largest absolute component. Say which norm a 'distance' or a 'clip' uses |
| 12 | Observance of the Proxy Metric | Optimise a number hard enough and it stops measuring what it once did. Ask today whether your metric still tracks the outcome anyone cares about, or has itself become the goal |
| 13 | Commemoration of Saint Seasona the Expectant | A series with a daily or weekly cycle has peaks that are normal. Seasona removed the trend and the seasonal pattern first and looked for anomalies in what remained. Without that step every Monday morning peak raises an alert |
| 14 | Feast of the Fine-Tuned Spirit | A pretrained machine spirit already knows edges, grammar and a great deal about the world. Fine-tuning asks it to learn only your task, which is why a few thousand examples can suffice |
| 15 | Rite of the Histogram | The histogram shows what the mean and the standard deviation conceal: two peaks, a long tail, a spike at zero. Draw one for every numeric column, and try more than one bin width |
| 16 | Vigil of the Label Noise | Some labels in almost every dataset are simply wrong. Keep watch over the examples where a good model disagrees most confidently with its label; many of them are errors in the data, not in the model |
| 17 | Observance of the Floating Point | In binary floating point, 0.1 plus 0.2 does not equal 0.3. Compare floats within a tolerance, never by strict equality, and count money as integers in its smallest unit |
